## 2. Per-item audit

### Lemma 1 (denominator forcing). Verdict: VERIFIED, fully.

Claim: a solution of H(a,b) + 1/m in Z forces m = B and B | (A+1); at most one candidate m per interval.
Re-derivation: if A/B + 1/m = k in Z, then A/B = (km - 1)/m, and gcd(km - 1, m) = gcd(1, m) = 1, so (km - 1)/m is a reduced fraction with denominator m; by uniqueness of lowest terms m = B, and then A/B = (kB - 1)/B gives A = kB - 1, i.e. A congruent to -1 mod B. The note's proof is exactly this and is complete and correct, including the edge cases (B = 1 gives m = 1; positivity gives k >= 1, and km - 1 = 0 would force H = 0, impossible). The preliminary display (2) in Section 2 of the note, B | m(A+1), is stated without derivation; it is true (from Bm | Am + B one gets B | Am, hence B | m since gcd(A,B) = 1, hence B | m(A+1)), but it is not load-bearing, since the proof of Lemma 1 does not use it. Recorded as minor gap G4 below, not as an error.

### Lemma 2, case (a) (T_p not 0 mod p). Verdict: VERIFIED, with one formally misstated step in the note's proof (defect D3; the conclusion and the fix are both correct).

Setup verified: p^2 > b gives floor(b/p) < p, so u + k - 1 <= floor(b/p) < p and u >= 1, hence p does not divide any u + j; every multiple of p in [a,b] has exact valuation 1; the complementary sum U over n coprime to p is p-adically integral, as is T_p; and H(a,b) = U + (1/p) T_p exactly.
Valuation: T_p not 0 mod p and T_p p-integral give v_p(T_p) = 0, so the second term has valuation -1 while U has valuation >= 0; by the ultrametric property v_p(H) = -1 exactly, so v_p(den H) = 1, and Lemma 1 (which assumes a solution) gives v_p(m) = 1. Correct.
Congruence: write m = p*m1, p not dividing m1. From H + 1/m = N in Z, multiply by p: pH + 1/m1 = pN. Since pU is in pZ_p, pH is congruent to T_p mod pZ_p, so T_p + m1^{-1} = 0 in F_p, m1 = -(T_p mod p)^{-1} mod p, and m = p*m1 is therefore congruent to -p*(T_p mod p)^{-1} mod p^2. The formula and the modulus p^2 are correct (m1 mod p determines p*m1 mod p^2, regardless of the lift of the inverse).
The misstated step: the note's "for completeness" paragraph says that (1/p)T_p + 1/(p*m1) is 0 "in Z_p / pZ_p". Both terms have valuation -1 and are not elements of Z_p, so that congruence is formally meaningless as written. The very next displayed consequence, T_p + m1^{-1} congruent to 0 mod p, is the correct statement and is exactly what multiplication by p yields. This is an exposition defect in one intermediate display, repaired in one line; it does not affect the result. The first bullet of the proof ("Reducing the equality modulo p in Z_p yields (4)") is the same informality in compressed form, with the completeness paragraph supplying the real argument.

### Lemma 2, case (b) (T_p = 0 mod p, the bad prime). Verdict: VERIFIED, fully.

T_p = 0 mod p with T_p p-integral gives v_p(T_p) >= 1, so (1/p)T_p is p-integral, H = U + (1/p)T_p is p-integral, v_p(H) >= 0, p does not divide den H, and by Lemma 1 p does not divide m, i.e. v_p(m) = 0. No cancellation subtlety arises, since both summands are integral regardless of their residues. The further statement that the prime "contributes no modulus-p^2 congruence" is correct as a statement about this construction: the dichotomy gives no congruence for m mod p^2 from a bad prime. (The restriction to odd p is unnecessary under the hypothesis p^2 > b, where the same argument works for p = 2, but dropping p = 2 is harmless and is explained in the note's Remark 9.)

### Lemma 5 (T(u) = D'(u)/D(u); at most k-1 bad u in F_p). Verdict: VERIFIED, with a minor under-justification (gap G1).

In F_p[u], D(u) = prod_{j=0}^{k-1} (u+j), and the logarithmic derivative identity D'/D = sum_j 1/(u+j) = T(u) holds wherever D(u) is nonzero, by direct differentiation. Hence, on the stated domain (no factor u+j equal to 0 in F_p), T(u) = 0 iff D'(u) = 0. Under the hypothesis 1 <= k < p, the leading coefficient of D' is k, which is nonzero in F_p, so deg D' is exactly k - 1 (the note asserts deg D' = k - 1 without noting that this uses k not congruent to 0 mod p; the hypothesis k < p supplies it). A nonzero polynomial of degree k - 1 over the field F_p has at most k - 1 roots, so at most k - 1 values of u in F_p are bad. Two points the note does not mention, both harmless: D' is not the zero polynomial (again because k is nonzero mod p), and D and D' share no root (the roots of D are distinct mod p since k <= p), so no root of D' falls outside the domain of T. Computationally confirmed in full (Section 4): for every odd prime p <= 120 and every 1 <= k <= min(p-1, 12) (326 (p,k) cases, all u in F_p), the bad u are exactly the roots of D', and their number is at most k - 1, with 0 failures.

### Corollary 6 (fixed (u,k): finitely many bad primes), including the k = 2 classification and the three u = 1 cases. Verdict: VERIFIED, with a minor under-justification (gap G2).

Write T_{u,k} = N_{u,k}/M_{u,k} in lowest terms. For a prime p > u + k - 1, p divides no u + j, and the reduced denominator M_{u,k} (which divides lcm(u, ..., u+k-1), all of whose prime factors are at most u + k - 1, hence less than p) is also prime to p; the note's proof does not state this p-not-divides-M step explicitly, but it holds, and then T_{u,k} mod p = N_{u,k} * M_{u,k}^{-1} in F_p, which is 0 iff p | N_{u,k}. Since T_{u,k} > 0, N_{u,k} is a fixed nonzero integer with finitely many prime divisors, so only finitely many primes p > u + k - 1 are bad for the fixed pair (u,k); the remaining primes p <= u + k - 1 are finitely many trivially. Correct as stated in the note, with the restriction p > u + k - 1 properly present in its statement.
k = 2 classification: T_p = 1/u + 1/(u+1) = (2u + 1)/(u(u+1)); for odd p, with u, u+1 nonzero mod p (guaranteed in the Lemma 2 regime by u + 1 < p), this is 0 mod p iff 2u + 1 = 0 mod p iff u = (p-1)/2, the symmetric pair. The note's derivation is correct. Computationally confirmed for all odd primes p <= 500 and all u with u + 1 < p: bad iff u = (p-1)/2, 0 failures.
The three u = 1 cases (the note's Remark 7): T_{1,1} = 1, numerator 1, never bad; T_{1,2} = 3/2, numerator 3, bad only at p = 3; T_{1,3} = 11/6, numerator 11, bad only at p = 11. All three are exact and correct (for k = 3, directly: mod 11 the sum is 1 + 6 + 4 = 11 = 0, using 2^{-1} = 6 and 3^{-1} = 4 mod 11). Computationally, for all odd primes p <= 500 with p > u + k - 1, the bad sets are exactly the empty set, {3}, and {11} respectively, and in each case equal the set of prime divisors of the numerator, as Corollary 6 predicts.

### Proposition 8 (a <= b/3: primes in (b/3, b] are good; log Q bound). Verdict: VERIFIED.

Assume b >= 10 and a <= b/3. For a prime p with b/3 < p <= b: p > b/3 > sqrt(b) for b > 9, so p^2 > b; a <= b/3 < p and a >= 1 give u = ceil(a/p) = 1; the multiples of p up to b are p and possibly 2p, so k = 1 for p > b/2 and k = 2 for b/3 < p <= b/2 (endpoint p = b/2 included correctly, since then 2p = b). The k = 1 case has T_p = 1, always good; the k = 2, u = 1 case is bad only at p = 3 by the case above. Hence every such prime is in P1(a,b), the product of p^2 over them divides Q(a,b), and log Q(a,b) >= 2(theta(b) - theta(b/3)) - O(1), where the O(1) (at most 2 log 3) covers the stated exclusion of p = 3. Two cosmetic observations, neither an error: (i) under the hypothesis b >= 10 the excluded case p = 3 cannot actually occur, since p > b/3 >= 10/3 > 3, so the exception "p not equal to 3" is vacuous and the bound holds with no O(1) loss; (ii) P1(a,b) is used here before its formal definition in Section 4 of the note. Computationally confirmed: for all b <= 500 and all a <= b/3, no prime in (b/3, b] is bad, 0 failures.

### Proposition 10 (a <= b/2 regime: primes in (b/2, b] are good; log Q >= 2(theta(b) - theta(b/2))). Verdict: the goodness claim and the congruence contribution VERIFIED; the displayed theta lower bound is FALSE as stated for b = 2 and b = 3 (defect D2), and correct for b >= 4. The asymptotic consequence is unaffected.

The note's hypothesis is a <= floor(b/2) + 1 (slightly weaker than a <= b/2; the audit follows the note). For an odd prime p with b/2 < p <= b: 2p > b, so at most one multiple of p lies in [a,b], and p >= floor(b/2) + 1 >= a (the smallest prime exceeding b/2 is at least floor(b/2) + 1), so p itself lies in [a,b], k = 1, u = 1, T_p = 1, nonzero mod p. The prime is good and contributes its congruence. This part is fully correct, and was confirmed computationally for all b <= 500 and all a <= floor(b/2) + 1 (0 bad primes in the range).
The bound as printed, log Q(a,b) >= 2 * sum_{b/2 < p <= b} log p = 2(theta(b) - theta(b/2)), identifies the sum over odd primes in (b/2, b] with the full theta difference. That identification fails exactly when the prime 2 lies in (b/2, b], i.e. for b = 2 and b = 3, since Q is built from odd primes only. Concrete counterexamples, both satisfying the hypothesis: (a,b) = (2,3) (the note's own worked example) has Q = 9, log Q = 2 log 3 = 2.197, while 2(theta(3) - theta(3/2)) = 2(log 2 + log 3) = 3.584; (a,b) = (1,2) has Q = 1, log Q = 0, while the right side is 2 log 2 = 1.386. A computational probe over all b <= 12 found violations at b = 2 and b = 3 only. Repair: add the hypothesis b >= 4, or sum over odd primes, or subtract O(1) as Proposition 8 does. Remark 11's consequence, log Q >= (1 + o(1)) b for a <= b/2, follows correctly for large b from theta(b) - theta(b/2) = (1/2 + o(1)) b by the prime number theorem (the o(1) in the lower bound is the standard unsigned shorthand; (1 - o(1)) b would be the pedantically signed form).

### The CRT reduction (m in one class mod Q; m | lcm(a..b); m b-smooth). Verdict: VERIFIED, with one minor under-justification (gap G3).

The moduli p^2 for distinct good primes are pairwise coprime, so the congruences of Lemma 2(a) combine by the Chinese remainder theorem into a unique class m = r(a,b) mod Q(a,b); if P1(a,b) is empty, Q = 1 and the class is trivial, which is consistent. From Lemma 1, m = den H(a,b); writing H(a,b) with the common denominator L = lcm(a..b) shows the reduced denominator divides L, hence m | L and m <= L. The note asserts "m | lcm(a..b)" directly from m = den H without this one-line justification (gap G3), but the step is standard and correct. Every prime divisor of m then divides L, so every prime divisor of m is at most b: m is b-smooth (with m = 1 b-smooth vacuously). The note's warning that log lcm(a..b) is not psi(b) - psi(a-1), with the example that [4,5] still contributes 2^2, and its corrected formula log lcm(a..b) = sum_{p <= b} e_p(a,b) log p with e_p(a,b) = max{k >= 0 : floor(b/p^k) - floor((a-1)/p^k) >= 1}, are both correct.

### Theorem 13 (global target lemma). Verdict: the implication is logically correct, BUT its hypothesis is unsatisfiable as quantified, so the theorem is vacuous as a target (defect D1, the most serious finding of this audit).

What is correct: the proof itself is sound. If a solution had b >= B_0, Lemma 1 and (6) would make its m b-smooth with m <= lcm(a..b), and the CRT construction would place m in the forced class, contradicting the assumed exclusion; the finitely many pairs with b < B_0 contribute at most one m each by Lemma 1. As a conditional implication, nothing is wrong.
Why the hypothesis can never hold: it is required for EVERY b >= B_0 and EVERY 1 <= a <= b. Take a = b = q^2 for any odd prime q (arbitrarily large, so no choice of B_0 avoids these pairs). The interval is the singleton {q^2}. An odd prime p with p^2 > q^2 has p > q, and a multiple of p in {q^2} would force p | q^2, i.e. p = q, contradiction; hence P1(q^2, q^2) is empty, Q = 1, and the "forced class" mod 1 is the class of all integers. That class contains m = 1, which is b-smooth (it has no prime factors, and the note's own phrasing of condition (i), "all prime factors of m are <= b", is vacuously true for m = 1) and satisfies 1 <= lcm(a..b) = q^2. So the assumed exclusion fails for (q^2, q^2) for every prime q. The antecedent of Theorem 13 is therefore false for every B_0, the implication is vacuously true, and, as a "global target lemma" to be proved en route to finiteness, it is unusable as stated: no future argument can establish a false hypothesis. (Under the alternative reading that r(a,b) is undefined when P1 is empty, the hypothesis is instead ill-formed for those pairs; either reading is a defect of formulation, not of the underlying sieve.) A repair would need to restrict the quantification, for example to pairs (a,b) for which the forced class is defined by additional data (including Lemma 1's condition A = -1 mod B, or the 2-adic and small-prime information the sieve discards), or to require the exclusion only for pairs where P1(a,b) is nonempty together with a separate disposal of the P1-empty pairs. Note that the P1-empty pairs are not themselves solutions (for (q^2,q^2), H = 1/q^2 and Lemma 1 already excludes a solution, since q^2 does not divide 2); the defect is that Theorem 13's stated hypothesis demands more than the CRT data can ever deliver, not that any solution escapes. Computationally confirmed for q in {3, 5, 7, 11, 13}: P1(q^2, q^2) is empty in each case (Section 4).
This defect does not touch Lemmas 1, 2, 5, Corollary 6, Propositions 8 and 10, or the CRT reduction, which are per-interval statements. It does mean the note's framing of Theorem 13 as "the" global statement whose proof would finish the singleton case overstates what is on the table: a correct target lemma of this shape has yet to be formulated.

### Remark 14 (Q > lcm alone does not suffice). Verdict: VERIFIED, fully.

The argument is correct: if Q > X and 0 < m <= X with m = r mod Q, then m must equal the least positive representative r_0 of the class (with r_0 in {1, ..., Q}, the class 0 represented by Q itself), and r_0 <= X is not excluded by size alone; hence one needs r_0 > lcm(a..b) or r_0 not b-smooth, and any implication of the form "Q much larger than lcm, therefore r_0 > lcm or r_0 non-smooth" would indeed supply the exclusion Theorem 13 asks for (for the pairs where the class is nontrivial; see D1). The note's own worked example (2,3) is a concrete witness the remark is right: Q(2,3) = 9 > lcm(2,3) = 6, yet r_0 = 6 <= 6 is 3-smooth and is an actual solution, so Q > lcm provably cannot rule solutions out in general.

## 3. Worked-example verification (exact computation)

Example (a,b) = (2,3): H(2,3) = 1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6 exactly as printed; Lemma 1 gives A = 5, B = 6, (A+1) mod B = 0, m = 6, k = 1. Prime p = 3: p^2 = 9 > 3, k(3) = 1, u = 1, T_3 = 1, good; forced congruence m = -3 * 1^{-1} = -3 = 6 mod 9, and 6 mod 9 = 6. P1(2,3) = {3}, Q = 9, forced class m = 6 mod 9. Every printed claim in the note's Section 6.1 is verified.

Example (a,b) = (3,6): the note's Section 6.2 prints H(3,6) = 49/20. Exact computation gives H(3,6) = 1/3 + 1/4 + 1/5 + 1/6 = 19/20. The printed numerator 49 is wrong (defect D4 below): taken literally, 49/20 would give (49 + 1) mod 20 = 10, so Lemma 1 would yield no solution at all, contradicting the same paragraph. With the true value 19/20, everything else in the paragraph checks out exactly: A = 19, B = 20, (A + 1) mod B = 0, m = 20, k = 1. Primes: p = 3 has u = 1, k = 2, T_3 = 3/2 = 0 mod 3, bad, and indeed v_3(20) = 0, so 3 does not divide m, as Lemma 2(b) predicts; p = 5 has u = 1, k = 1, T_5 = 1, good, forced congruence m = -5 * 1^{-1} = 20 mod 25, and 20 mod 25 = 20, with v_5(20) = 1 as Lemma 2(a) predicts. P1(3,6) = {5}, Q = 25, forced class m = 20 mod 25. All of the example's conclusions are correct; only the displayed value of H(3,6) is misprinted.

## 5. Defect and gap list

Defects (statements that are wrong, or formally invalid, as printed):

- D1 (major; formulation). Theorem 13's hypothesis is unsatisfiable as quantified: for (a,b) = (q^2, q^2), q any odd prime, P1 is empty, Q = 1, the forced class is all integers, and m = 1 is a b-smooth positive integer with 1 <= lcm(a..b) in that class, so the required exclusion fails for arbitrarily large b and every B_0. The implication is vacuously true but the "global target lemma" cannot serve as a sufficient target as stated. Details and repair directions in the Theorem 13 item above. No other item depends on Theorem 13, so the damage is confined to the note's closing framing.
- D2 (minor; small cases). Proposition 10's displayed bound log Q(a,b) >= 2(theta(b) - theta(b/2)) is false for b = 2 and b = 3, because it counts the prime 2, which never contributes to Q. Counterexamples: (a,b) = (2,3), log Q = log 9 = 2.197 < 3.584 = 2(theta(3) - theta(3/2)); (a,b) = (1,2), log Q = 0 < 2 log 2. True for b >= 4; the asymptotic Remark 11 is unaffected.
- D3 (minor; proof exposition). In Lemma 2's proof, the congruence "(1/p)T_p + 1/(p*m1) = 0 in Z_p / pZ_p" is formally invalid, since both terms have valuation -1 and do not lie in Z_p. The intended and correct step is multiplication of the integrality equation by p, yielding T_p + m1^{-1} = 0 mod p, which is what the note uses immediately afterward. Conclusion unaffected.
- D4 (minor; misprint with literal consequences). Section 6.2 prints H(3,6) = 49/20; the correct value is 19/20. Read literally, the printed value violates Lemma 1's condition (20 does not divide 49 + 1 = 50) and would give no solution, contradicting the example's own conclusions, which are all correct for 19/20.

Gaps (correct steps whose justification is missing or compressed in the note; no repair changes any conclusion):

- G1. Lemma 5 asserts deg D' = k - 1 without noting this needs the leading coefficient k to be nonzero in F_p, supplied by the hypothesis 1 <= k < p; the same point gives D' not identically zero.
- G2. Corollary 6's equivalence "T_{u,k} = 0 mod p iff p | N_{u,k}" needs the reduced denominator M_{u,k} to be prime to p; true because M_{u,k} divides lcm(u, ..., u+k-1) and p > u + k - 1, but not stated.
- G3. The CRT section's "m | lcm(a..b)" is asserted from m = den H(a,b) without the common-denominator argument (H(a,b) = (integer)/lcm(a..b), so the reduced denominator divides lcm(a..b)).
- G4. Section 2's display (2), B | m(A+1), is stated without derivation; it is true but unused by the proof of Lemma 1.
- G5 (cosmetic). Proposition 8's exception "p not equal to 3" is vacuous under its own hypothesis b >= 10 (any p > b/3 then exceeds 3), and the -O(1) in its log Q bound is accordingly unnecessary; P1(a,b) is used in Proposition 8 before being defined in Section 4.

Explicit statement on the core mathematics: apart from D1 to D4 and the gaps G1 to G5 above, no defect, gap, or under-justified step was found. In particular, Lemma 1, both cases of Lemma 2, Lemma 5, Corollary 6 with its k = 2 and u = 1 classifications, the range-goodness parts of Propositions 8 and 10, the CRT reduction, and Remark 14 are correct, and Lemma 2's dichotomy survived an exhaustive exact test over 45,150 intervals and 1,434,498 prime incidences with zero failures. The note does not claim to prove finiteness, and this audit confirms that self-assessment; the one place where the note's framing exceeds what its statements deliver is Theorem 13 (D1).
